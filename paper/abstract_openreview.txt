Post-training ternary quantization (1.91 bits/weight at the model level; 1.69 for the TQ1_0 plane format itself) makes very large mixture-of-experts models runnable on consumer unified-memory hardware: we quantize a 397B-parameter, 512-expert MoE to 88 GiB and a 35B sibling to 9.2 GiB, both served by a modified llama.cpp with Vulkan ternary kernels. We then study a corrective mechanism — a second ternary plane on the hottest experts — that halves weight reconstruction error (46% → 22%) and halves the single-expert output error under real activations. Applied uniformly, the correction makes both models worse. Per-layer, per-projection engine switches localise the damage and show that everything is decided by where the correction lands, not by how faithful it is: the identical perturbation, delivered at identical relative magnitude to every layer's output, improves the model in the final third of the depth and is catastrophic near the middle (a single layer moves perplexity from 8.72 to 37.8; the same correction at the last layers improves it). The sign flips in the final third on both models — between 0.63 and 0.73 of depth on the 35B and between 0.50 and 0.73 on the 397B, at the resolution of the band search — across a 10× size difference. Eight candidate explanations, each pre-registered as a falsifiable prediction, are refuted, among them correction magnitude, routing concentration and the delivered output error itself; route-flip counts anti-correlate with damage. The beneficial configuration is confirmed by a paired logit-level comparison (mean Δp = +0.048% ± 0.014%) and ships as a file rather than a runtime flag. We then evaluate the shipped 397B file against the closest-size public quantization of the same checkpoint (IQ1_M, 1.84 bits/weight, 3.5% smaller) on nine complete public benchmark corpora and a declared subset of a tenth, with the exact commands: the ternary file wins BoolQ (+7.99 points), HellaSwag (+2.33) and MMLU (+2.13) with disjoint 95% intervals, shows no negative difference beyond its interval on any corpus, and is indistinguishable on WikiText-2 perplexity (7.5180 against 7.5173) — the cheapest and most widely reported of these measurements is the only one that cannot tell the two files apart. On an internal paired suite of 143 coding-and-agentic trials (aggregate results only) the same file resolves 78.3% against 55.2% (exact McNemar p = 5.42e-7). Two further measurements accompany the main result: a GPTQ-calibrated forge that wins with reasoning off but degenerates into termination-token loops with reasoning on, where an RTN variant at the same bit-rate reasons correctly; and the measured distribution of the MoE top-k routing margin, which to our knowledge prior work gives only as a definition or a token-averaged mean (median 10× narrower than a uniform share; 2.95% of tokens within one bf16 ULP of a routing flip).
