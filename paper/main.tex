\begin{abstract}
Post-training ternary quantization (1.91 bits/weight at the model level;
1.69 for the TQ1\_0 plane format itself \citep{compilade2024tq1}) makes very large
mixture-of-experts models runnable on consumer unified-memory hardware: we
quantize a 397B-parameter, 512-expert MoE to 88~GiB and a 35B sibling to
9.2~GiB, both served by a modified llama.cpp with Vulkan ternary kernels. We
then study a \emph{corrective} mechanism --- a second ternary plane on the
hottest experts --- that halves weight reconstruction error (46\% $\to$ 22\%)
and halves the single-expert output error under real activations.
\textbf{Applied uniformly, the correction makes both models worse.} Per-layer,
per-projection engine switches localise the damage and show that everything
is decided by \emph{where} the correction lands, not by \emph{how faithful} it
is: the identical perturbation, delivered at identical relative magnitude to
every layer's output, improves the model in the final third of the depth and
is catastrophic near the middle (a single layer moves perplexity from 8.72 to
37.8; the same correction at the last layers \emph{improves} it). The sign
flips in the final third on both models --- between 0.63 and 0.73 of depth on
the 35B and between 0.50 and 0.73 on the 397B, at the resolution of the band
search --- across a $10\times$ size difference. Eight candidate explanations,
each pre-registered as a falsifiable prediction, are refuted, among them
correction magnitude, routing concentration and the delivered output error
itself; route-flip counts \emph{anti-correlate} with damage. The beneficial
configuration is confirmed by a paired logit-level comparison (mean
$\Delta p = +0.048\% \pm 0.014\%$) and ships as a file rather than a runtime
flag. We then evaluate the shipped 397B file against the closest-size public
quantization of the same checkpoint (IQ1\_M, 1.84 bits/weight, 3.5\% smaller)
on nine complete public benchmark corpora and a declared subset of a tenth,
with the exact commands: the ternary file wins BoolQ ($+7.99$ points),
HellaSwag ($+2.33$) and MMLU ($+2.13$) with disjoint 95\% intervals, shows no
negative difference beyond its interval on any corpus, and is
\emph{indistinguishable} on WikiText-2 perplexity ($7.5180$ against
$7.5173$) --- the cheapest and most widely reported of these measurements is
the only one that cannot tell the two files apart. On an internal paired
suite of 143 coding-and-agentic trials (aggregate results only) the same file
resolves 78.3\% against 55.2\% (exact McNemar $p = 5.42\times 10^{-7}$). Two
further measurements accompany the main result: a GPTQ-calibrated forge that
wins with reasoning off but degenerates into termination-token loops with
reasoning on, where an RTN variant at the same bit-rate reasons correctly;
and the measured \emph{distribution} of the MoE top-$k$ routing margin, which
to our knowledge prior work gives only as a definition or a token-averaged
mean (median $10\times$ narrower than a uniform share; 2.95\% of tokens within
one bf16 ULP of a routing flip).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A 397-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model does not fit in the memory
of any consumer machine at the precisions its authors shipped. At 1.91
bits/weight (1.69 for the ternary planes themselves) it does: 88~GiB, resident
in the unified memory of a desktop APU, served by ternary Vulkan kernels
that are now part of upstream llama.cpp. This paper is
about what happened when we tried to make that model \emph{better} --- and measured,
twice, that the intuitive way to do so makes it worse.

The corrective mechanism is a second ternary plane on the most-routed
experts: a residual quantization that demonstrably halves weight
reconstruction error (46\% $\to$ 22\%) and halves the single-expert output error
under the layer's own captured activations. Every local metric of fidelity
improves. Applied uniformly across the network, perplexity degrades --- on
both a 35B and a 397B model of the same family. This is the correction
paradox, and it is not a bug: we verified the file layout, the expert
permutations (28/28 correct on all 40 MoE layers), the scales, and the delivered
error, and then spent the balance of the work localising \emph{where} the paradox
lives.

The answer is depth. Per-layer engine switches (enabling the correction for
arbitrary layer ranges at load time, without re-forging) show that the same
perturbation, delivered at the same relative magnitude to every layer's
output, improves the model in the final third of the network and is
catastrophic near the middle: one single mid-depth layer moves perplexity
from 8.72 to 37.8, while the correction restricted to the tail improves on
the uncorrected baseline. On both models the sign flips in the final third of
the depth (35B: between 0.63 and 0.73; 397B: between 0.50 and 0.73, at the
resolution of the band search), at a $10\times$ parameter difference, and the
shipped bands begin at 73\% of depth on both. Eight
candidate explanations, each pre-registered as a falsifiable prediction
before its measurement ran, are refuted in Section~\ref{sec:refutations}; what
survives is a statement about position, not fidelity.
Section~\ref{sec:depthrule} turns the statement into a recipe that ships
inside the file. Section~\ref{sec:margin} reports a measurement the
routing-stability literature has so far assumed: the distribution of the
top-$k$ routing margin, whose skew explains why the one published mean curve
misleads.

We write from a deployment setting the quantization literature rarely
occupies --- one consumer machine for the forge and the per-layer anatomy, no
cluster, no fp16 reference for the largest model; cloud GPUs only for the
complete public benchmarks of Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} --- and we are
explicit throughout about what that setting does to
measurement: paired estimators where absolute error bars would drown the
effect, and functional probes where benchmark harnesses disagree with each
other. Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} reports nine public benchmark
corpora run in their entirety and a declared subset of a tenth, on the shipped
397B file and its closest-size public rival, and on a 35B sibling and its
rival, with sources, item counts, commands and hardware; the 397B numbers are
regenerable from public files, the 35B file is not yet public
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). This is a revised version of a previously
submitted manuscript; the changes, and the corrections they carry, are listed
in Section~\ref{sec:changes}.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A depth rule, measured twice.} A partial ternary correction helps only
   in the final third of the network, with the sign flipping in overlapping
   depth brackets (35B: 0.63--0.73; 397B: 0.50--0.73) on two MoE models
   $10\times$ apart in size, and the shipped bands beginning at 73\% of depth
   on both. The recipe that
   follows --- correction baked into the file for the tail only --- improves the
   35B by 5.4\% and the 397B measurably (paired, $3.4\sigma$), with no functional
   regression on verifiable-answer probes.
\item \textbf{Eight pre-registered refutations.} Every local account of the damage is
   excluded by measurement, including the decisive one: the perturbation
   \emph{delivered to the layer's output}, $\|\Delta W \cdot X\|/\|W X\|$ under that layer's own
   captured activations, is constant across depth (1.000--1.002 ratio to the
   weight-space figure) and slightly \emph{smaller} where damage is greatest
   ($\rho = -0.599$). Route-flip counts nearly invert the damage ordering
   (all-layers: $5\times$ the flips of the worst single layer, $3.5\times$ less damage).
\item \textbf{Complete public benchmarks at matched size.} On nine public
   benchmark corpora run whole and a declared 1,000-item PIQA subset
   (Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks}), the shipped
   397B file beats a 3.5\% smaller IQ1\_M quantization of the same checkpoint
   on BoolQ ($+7.99$ points), HellaSwag ($+2.33$) and MMLU ($+2.13$) with
   disjoint 95\% intervals --- and, under the paired per-item test, also on
   ARC-Easy ($+2.48$, $p = 0.002$) at the Bonferroni-corrected level --- shows
   no negative difference beyond its interval on any corpus, and is
   indistinguishable on WikiText-2 perplexity (7.5180 against 7.5173). The 397B
   numbers are regenerable from public files, the printed commands and the
   released converter (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}); the engine source is
   archived as stated in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. Perplexity and
   Winogrande of the 397B pair and HellaSwag of the 35B file were re-measured
   on a second GPU vendor and agree within one combined standard error
   (Section~\ref{sec:crosscheck}, which also declares one paired backend
   shift); the three headline separations have no second-vendor measurement.
\item \textbf{Task-level evidence at matched size, and a quantizer-level
   dissociation.} On an internal paired suite (two fixed seeds, 143 paired
   trials, identical server, reasoning off), the shipped file resolves 78.3\%
   against 55.2\% for a slightly \emph{smaller} IQ1\_M quantization of the
   same model (85.1 vs.\ 88.2~GiB; the size handicap favours the baseline)
   (discordant pairs 39--6, exact McNemar $p = 5.4\times 10^{-7}$; paired
   bootstrap CI$_{95}$ of the difference $[+14.7, +31.5]$ points). With
   reasoning \emph{on}, the ranking inverts: the GPTQ-calibrated planes
   degenerate into termination-token loops while an RTN forge at the same
   bit-rate passes every reasoning probe --- a dissociation between
   quantization algorithms that neither perplexity nor no-think task rate
   detects (Section~\ref{sec:tasks}).
\item \textbf{The routing-margin distribution.} Defined in prior work (ReMoE's
   $\gamma$ in probability space; a logit-space ``router margin'' reduced to one AUC)
   and, to our knowledge, reported only as a token-averaged mean
   \citep{chen2026certainhead}: we give percentiles. Median $10.1\times$ narrower than the
   uniform expert share; 1st percentile $806\times$ narrower; 2.95\% of tokens
   within one bf16 ULP. The distribution is skewed (mean/median 1.76), which
   is precisely why the published token-averaged mean misleads.
\item \textbf{An engineering result.} A forge that produces two-plane
   ternary MoE files from bf16 on one consumer GPU (7.6~h for 397B), a strip
   tool that bakes measured layer profiles into files, and a two-plane loader
   with per-layer switches on top of the TQ1\_0 Vulkan kernels that are now
   part of upstream llama.cpp (the loader is released as a diff against
   identified upstream commits in the supplementary material,
   Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What we explicitly do not claim}

\begin{itemize}
\item Not ``more bits hurt'': the claim is \emph{heterogeneous} fidelity hurts, and the
  literature contains the same phenomenon unremarked (Table~1 of GEMQ \citep{gemq}: expert-level
  mixed precision losing to uniform at matched bits; HiFloat4:
  restoring one component to high precision scoring below uniform FP4 \citep{mak2026hifloat4}).
\item Not the concept of the routing margin (ReMoE \citep{zhu2026remoe} defines
  it), nor the down-projection bottleneck (D$^2$Quant \citep{yan2026d2quant} calls it ``well-known''),
  nor non-additivity of compression error (EvoPress \citep{sieberling2025evopress} opens on
  it). Our claims are the measurements, the localisation, and the rule.
\item Not a mechanism. Eleven hypotheses died; the depth rule and the
  observation that intermediate layers host a shared semantic representation
  \citep{wu2025semantichub} are consistent (our worst layer sits at 0.488 of
  depth), but consistency is not identification. We state the open problem as
  narrowly as the measurements allow.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup and measurement honesty}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Hardware.} Everything except the whole-corpus benchmark runs of
Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} runs on one consumer machine: Ryzen AI Max+ 395,
128~GB unified memory ($\sim$96~GiB exposed as Vulkan VRAM), Fedora 43 (Linux
7.0.8, Mesa 25.3.6 RADV), our llama.cpp
fork with TQ1\_0 Vulkan kernels (scalar, coopmat1 and coopmat2 paths). A
second consumer laptop (RTX~4060 Laptop 8~GB)
serves as an auxiliary bench for the 35B model; no number in this paper comes
from it. The benchmark runs of Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} were made on cloud
GPUs (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\paragraph{Models and forge.} The 397B (512 experts $\times$ 60 layers, hybrid GatedDeltaNet
attention) is forged from the bf16 checkpoint: expert tensors ternarized with
a two-level optimal-scale search, either round-to-nearest (RTN) or with true
GPTQ \citep{gptq} against per-layer input
Hessians (16{,}640 samples each); non-expert tensors from an existing shelf
quantization. 7.6~h end-to-end. The 35B sibling is forged the same way from
a shelf GGUF. Engine switches allow enabling the second plane per layer range
and per projection at load time, which is what makes the per-layer anatomy
measurable without re-forging. The provenance of each benchmarked file is
stated in Section~\ref{sec:rival}.

\paragraph{What our perplexity numbers are, and are not.} All perplexity is
llama.cpp's on WikiText-2 raw; context length and chunk count are stated per
table. The per-layer and paired measurements of
Sections~\ref{sec:paradox}--\ref{sec:depthrule} use ctx 2048 and 30 chunks;
the full-corpus runs of Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} and of the ladder below
use ctx 4096 and all 72 chunks. Absolute error bars at 30 chunks are
$\pm 0.087$, so any comparison inside that band is resolved with the
\emph{paired} estimator (\texttt{--kl-divergence-base}): same chunks,
reference logits stored, uncertainty propagated from the logits. The headline
confirmation (tail-only correction on the 397B) is paired: mean
$\Delta p = +0.048\% \pm 0.014\%$.

\paragraph{A benchmark ladder, and where perplexity sits on it.}
With complete runs on both files we can order the evaluations by how closely
each resembles the task a deployed model performs, and read the gap along that
order. The rows below are the same pair of files and the same engine; the
public benchmarks (Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks}) were run whole on one GPU
type with the ternary file's second plane applied on all layers, the task
suite (Section~\ref{sec:tasks}) on another with it disabled
(Section~\ref{sec:rival}), and the ternary
file is the 3.6\% \emph{larger} of the two. Multiple-choice figures are
llama.cpp's per-token-normalised accuracy on the complete corpus with Wilson
95\% intervals; perplexity is WikiText-2 raw, ctx 4096, all 72 chunks. Gap
$=$ ours $-$ rival in points; for perplexity lower is better, and the
paired per-item exact McNemar $p$ is given where the logs allow it.

The gap is zero at token-level likelihood, between two and eight points on
multiple-choice completion (with disjoint 95\% intervals on HellaSwag, MMLU
and BoolQ; Winogrande's intervals overlap, and its four and a half points are
separated by the paired test at $p = 0.006$ --- nominally significant, just
above the Bonferroni-corrected threshold of $0.005$ over the ten corpora),
and twenty-three on multi-step coding and
agentic work. We do not claim a mechanism for the ordering; we report it
because the practical consequence is uncomfortable and easy to state:
\emph{the cheapest and most widely reported of these measurements is the only
one that cannot tell the two files apart.} The previous version of this paper
put the same sentence more strongly --- that perplexity ranks the pair
\emph{backwards}, on the strength of a home measurement of 7.5903 against
7.5438 whose Winogrande companions were $65.67\%$ against $61.09\%$
(one standard error $\pm 1.33$ and $\pm 1.37$; the same items give
$66.22\%$ against $61.64\%$ on the second GPU, with overlapping 95\%
intervals). That perplexity gap was inside its own
error bars and does not reproduce on the second GPU
(Section~\ref{sec:crosscheck}); the weaker statement is the one the data
support.

\paragraph{What our benchmark numbers are, and are not.} llama.cpp's built-in
scorers label their output \texttt{acc\_norm} but normalise endings by
\textbf{token count}; lm-eval's \texttt{acc\_norm} normalises by
\textbf{character length}, and for Winogrande and MMLU lm-eval has no
\texttt{acc\_norm} at all. Our multiple-choice figures are therefore a third
quantity, comparable between the four files of this paper and not with
published leaderboards; Section~\ref{sec:protocol} states the metric exactly.
What has changed since the previous version is the sample size: on complete
corpora the Wilson 95\% half-width is about 0.8 points on HellaSwag
($n = 10{,}042$) and 0.6 on MMLU ($n = 14{,}042$), so quantization-sized
effects of 1--4 points are resolvable, and we use these benchmarks as
measurements rather than as ``the model is not broken'' evidence. Generative
benchmarks (which require a harness against a server rather than
log-likelihood scoring of the served file) are stated as future work in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, and we explicitly do not use IRT-subsampled
minibenchmarks: the published gp-IRT weights put only 12--30\% of the reported
figure on responses actually observed, calibrated on a 2024 pool of
full-precision models that excludes the near-chance region a sub-2-bit model
can occupy --- a simulation on the published parameters shrinks true damage in
the flattering direction.

\paragraph{The task suite.} The public benchmarks above score log-likelihoods
of given choices; they do not measure multi-step, generative, tool-using work.
Task-level evidence on that construct comes from an internal private
evaluation suite (91
tasks in total; suite not publicly released). We report the 72 fixtures in the
seven families and legacy set that are unrelated to the deployment
environment --- runtime bug-fixing, refactoring, test authoring
(mutation-scored), long-context needle retrieval, tool use, deliberately
deceptive code, planning, and a legacy mixed set; the remaining tasks are
tied to the deploying household and withheld together with the suite itself.
Aggregate results only: the suite supports the \emph{paired} comparison
between two files run under identical conditions, and we make no
reproducibility claim on it --- reproducibility is promised only on the
public benchmarks of Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks}, where the published files
and the printed commands suffice. Each reported fixture is a
self-contained repository with target tests and regression tests; a trial
counts as resolved only if all target tests pass and no regression breaks
(tool-call fixtures are scored on the emitted call transcript against a
per-fixture contract). Every model runs every fixture at two fixed seeds (7
and 42), same llama.cpp server build, same 16{,}384-token context budget,
reasoning off --- 144 scheduled trials per model, of which 143 are valid
pairs, paired trial-for-trial, so
the comparison statistic is exact McNemar on the discordant pairs plus a
paired bootstrap (20{,}000 resamples) for the interval.

\paragraph{Pre-registration discipline.} Every hypothesis in Section~\ref{sec:refutations} was written as
a falsifiable prediction before its measurement ran, in the project log that
ships with the artefact. Two hypotheses were ``confirmed'' by a first
measurement and killed by a second (residual-stream rank; selection quality);
both stages are kept in the record.

\section{The paradox and the per-layer anatomy}
\label{sec:paradox}

\paragraph{The paradox, stated with its numbers.} On the 35B, the second plane halves
the weight reconstruction error of every expert it touches and halves the
single-expert output error under captured activations. Enabled everywhere:
perplexity 8.72 $\to$ 10.91. Every micro-level metric says the file got closer to
the source model; the model says otherwise.

\paragraph{Coarse localisation.} The per-projection switches split the damage
immediately: \texttt{up}-only 8.42 and \texttt{gate}-only 8.52 are both \emph{better} than
baseline, \texttt{up+gate} better still (8.35) --- and \texttt{down}-only alone is 11.24,
worse than everything enabled together. One projection carries all of it,
the same projection D$^2$Quant \citep{yan2026d2quant} calls a ``well-known bottleneck'' --- except here
the bottleneck is not low-bit difficulty but the \emph{correction itself} doing
harm.

\paragraph{Fine localisation.} Layer-range switches on \texttt{down} walk the damage to its
address: below the mountain (1--12) nearly harmless (9.02), above it (28--39)
neutral-to-helpful (8.71, and 8.36 for 30--39), the middle catastrophic ---
13--27 gives 25.34, and bisection lands on a single layer: \textbf{layer 20, alone,
8.72 $\to$ 37.80.} One of the 40 MoE layers, corrected --- by a mechanism that
demonstrably improves that layer's own reconstruction --- multiplies
perplexity by 4.3. Neighbouring layers step down smoothly (25: 22.5; 30:
8.69), so this is a region, not a defect in one tensor; the boundary-check
asserts and the permutation audit (Section~\ref{sec:refutations}, \#6) close the trivial
explanations.

\paragraph{The same anatomy at $10\times$.} The 397B repeats the shape with its own
geometry: the head is catastrophic, the middle harmful, and in the tail the
correction turns beneficial --- the band we ship begins at layer 44 of 60,
73\% of depth, as the 35B's begins at layer 30 of 41; the flip itself is
bracketed, not pinned, by the bands tested (Section~\ref{sec:depthrule}).
The magnitude scales down with model size (the 397B's
worst bands cost points, not multiples), which is itself consistent with the
redundancy argument the MoE-robustness literature makes; the \emph{sign
structure} is what transfers exactly.

\paragraph{Why ``paradox'' is the right word.} Sections~\ref{sec:refutations}--\ref{sec:depthrule} will show the perturbation
delivered to each layer's output is depth-invariant (ratio 1.000--1.002 to
the weight-space figure): the network is not receiving a bigger insult at
layer 20 --- it is receiving the \emph{same} insult at a depth that cannot absorb
it. Heterogeneous fidelity, not insufficient fidelity, is the failure mode.

\section{Eight refutations, each with its pre-registered prediction}
\label{sec:refutations}

a falsifiable prediction \emph{before} its measurement ran, and two of the eight
were ``confirmed'' by a first measurement and then killed by a second. We keep
both stages in the record, because the difference between a correlation and a
mechanism is precisely what this section is about.

Two cautionary subplots, reported rather than smoothed away:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The residual-stream rank correlation that did not replicate.} On the 35B,
  effective rank of the residual stream correlates with damage at $+0.651$ ---
  publishable-looking. On the 397B the sign \emph{reverses} (damage highest where
  rank is lowest). One model would have made this a mechanism; the second
  model made it a coincidence. We keep it as the section's epigraph.
\item \textbf{The selection hypothesis died twice.} Its correlational form is strong
  (impact lost by frequency-selection tracks the 397B damage profile at
  $+0.788$, and the head --- 2/28 correct, 89.5\% of impact lost --- is exactly the
  catastrophic band). Its causal form failed the pre-registered test above.
  Selection quality is a real, quantified property of the forge and it does
  not decide the sign. Both statements are true; conflating them is how the
  literature acquires mechanisms it does not have.
\end{itemize}

What survives all eight is a statement about \emph{position}: an identical
perturbation, delivered identically, has consequences that depend only on the
depth at which it lands --- beneficial in the final third (the shipped bands
begin at 73\% of depth on both models), catastrophic near the middle of the
35B (its worst layer sits at 0.488 of depth, in the intermediate region where
the representational literature locates a shared semantic hub
\citep{wu2025semantichub}), and progressively less harmful with depth on the
397B. Route-flip \emph{counts} do not order the damage (they nearly invert it), so
if routing mediates the effect it does so through \emph{which} routes change, not
how many --- consistent with the one published attempt to classify flips, which
achieves chance (AUC 0.490) at telling harmful from benign \citep{gu2026routeflip}.

\section{The depth rule and the recipe in the file}
\label{sec:depthrule}

\paragraph{The rule.} On both models, the second plane helps if and only if it is
restricted to the final third of the depth. On the 35B\footnote{The 35B has
41 layers counted 0--40; layer 40 is the multi-token-prediction layer and the
40 MoE layers are 0--39. Depth fractions in this paper use the denominator 41
for the 35B and 60 for the 397B.} the bands tested on \texttt{down} are 1--12
(9.02), 13--27 (25.34), 16--24 (19.06), 19--21 (10.08), 28--39 (8.71, against
8.72 uncorrected), 30--39 (8.36; 8.25 as the shipped recipe) and, in the sweep
with \texttt{up}+\texttt{gate} on everywhere (reference 8.35), 26--39 (9.80): bands starting at 28 are neutral and at 30 beneficial, a band
starting at 26 harms, so the sign flips between layers 26 and 30 --- between
0.63 and 0.73 of depth --- and the shipped band 30--39 begins at 73.2\%. On the
397B the bands tested are 0--19, 20--39 and 40--59 (a 12-chunk sweep in which
damage decreases monotonically with depth), 30--45 (harmful: 6.3002 against
6.1845 uncorrected at 30 chunks) and 44--59 (beneficial, paired, below): the
flip lies between layers 30 and 44 --- between 0.50 and 0.73 of depth --- and
the shipped band 44--59 begins at 73.3\%. Between the bands and
the mountains sits no gradual transition on the 35B --- layer 20 (0.488 of
depth) alone accounts for most of the uniform-application damage --- while
the 397B decays monotonically from the head (catastrophic, 2/28 selection
overlap) through the middle (harmful) to the tail (beneficial). That both flip
points fall in the final third of the depth, in overlapping brackets, across a
$10\times$ size difference and two different routing topologies, is the
paper's central empirical fact; the two-decimal agreement of the shipped
band edges (73.2\% and 73.3\%) is a property of the bands we chose to ship,
not a measured coincidence, and the band search resolves the flip no finer
than about $\pm 5\%$ of depth. We claim the measurement, not a law: two models,
one family (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{The recipe.} Because the engine switches that made the anatomy measurable
are ours, we could ship the rule as a runtime flag --- and explicitly do not.
A flag is a claim about every future file; a file is a claim about itself.
The forge takes \texttt{--plane2-layers LO-HI} and bakes the tail-only correction
in: plane-2 tensors exist only for the beneficial band, the expert
permutation and router reordering are applied only where the plane exists
(one predicate feeds all three coupled decisions), and a strip tool
retrofits the same profile onto already-forged files, reclaiming the dead
plane-2 bytes (4.1~GiB on the 397B). The resulting file runs on the
unmodified engine at full speed with no configuration.

\paragraph{Confirmation, 35B.} Tail-only (30--39) perplexity 8.2501 against 8.72
uncorrected --- a 5.4\% improvement --- where uniform application had \emph{degraded}
the same model. Functional probes (verifiable-answer arithmetic and
string-manipulation set) unchanged or improved; no regression observed.

\paragraph{Confirmation, 397B, paired.} At 397B the absolute error bar at 30 chunks
($\pm 0.087$) is larger than the expected effect, so the confirmation is paired
(\texttt{--kl-divergence-base}, same chunks, reference logits stored): mean
$\Delta p = +0.048\% \pm 0.014\%$ ($3.4\sigma$) for tail-only over uncorrected, improvement in
every evaluation chunk, and the verifiable-answer probes intact. The gain is
small and real; the point is its \emph{sign}, which uniform application gets
wrong at $10\times$ the magnitude.

\paragraph{Cost.} The tail-only file is strictly smaller than the uniform one (the
correction exists on 16 of the 60 layers rather than on all 60), loads
nothing extra at the head where it would do harm, and the depth rule
transfers across the two models tested at zero search cost --- against
EvoPress-style whole-model search \citep{sieberling2025evopress}, which subsumes the selection problem but
must re-run per model.

\section{Complete public benchmarks at matched size}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

The previous version of this paper reported HellaSwag on a 2,108-item random
subset and declared a complete standardised evaluation as a debt. This section
pays it: nine public benchmark corpora run in their entirety and a declared
1,000-item subset of a tenth (PIQA), on four files
--- the two 397B files compared in Section~\ref{sec:tasks} and a 35B sibling
with its own closest-size public rival --- with the exact sources, commands,
prompt templates and hardware. Every number in Table~\ref{tab:bench397} can be
regenerated from public files; Table~\ref{tab:bench35} cannot until the 35B
file is published (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Engine.} All runs use \texttt{llama-perplexity} from our llama.cpp
fork with the Vulkan backend. The single-plane TQ1\_0 Vulkan path is in
upstream llama.cpp (merged 2026-09-07); the second plane (the
\texttt{\_exps2} tensors and their application) lives only in the fork. A
two-plane file does \emph{not} load on upstream llama.cpp: we verified on an
unmodified upstream checkout (2026-09-06) that the loader rejects the 397B
file at load time (\texttt{done\_getting\_tensors: wrong number of tensors;
expected 1278, got 1098} --- the 180 unrecognised \texttt{\_exps2} tensors),
before any tensor data is read and independently of the backend. The fork is
therefore required to run the 397B file at all; only the single-plane
\emph{format} is upstream. No fork-specific environment variable is set in the
benchmark runs, and the fork applies a second plane on every layer whose
\texttt{\_exps2} tensors exist unless \texttt{ODINO\_P2\_LAYERS} or
\texttt{ODINO\_NO\_P2} restricts it; what that means for the shipped file is
stated in Section~\ref{sec:rival}. The fork's git history was not preserved
--- the working copies were made without it and the binary reports
\texttt{commit unknown} --- and the two-plane loader is not yet in a public
repository. The upstream commit the fork was branched from was identified
after the fact by matching every file the fork does not modify against the
upstream history (all of them are blob-identical to the tree of one commit of
2026-08-20, and a file changed by the next upstream commit is at its
pre-change state); relative to that commit the fork changes 19 files. The
engine used for every run is released with the supplementary material as two
diffs with their SHA-256: against that base commit, and against the upstream
merge commit of the TQ1\_0 Vulkan path (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Hardware.} The benchmark runs were executed on cloud NVIDIA RTX PRO
6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs (96~GiB; driver 595.91.07; Ubuntu 22.04;
Vulkan 1.4 through the NVIDIA ICD under a virtual X display, without which the
Vulkan loader silently falls back to a software rasteriser), one whole model per
GPU (\texttt{-ngl 999}), no CPU offload. The per-layer measurements of
Sections~\ref{sec:paradox}--\ref{sec:depthrule}, the previous version's
benchmark figures and the 35B HellaSwag home run were taken on the AMD desktop
APU (gfx1151, Mesa 25.3.6 RADV, Linux 7.0.8, Fedora 43) with the same fork
built from the same source tree; the RTX~4060 laptop of Section~\ref{sec:setup}
contributed no number.
Section~\ref{sec:crosscheck} checks the two GPU vendors against each other on
identical files and identical items.

\paragraph{Corpora.} Table~\ref{tab:corpora} lists every corpus with its
source, Hugging Face revision, split and item count. Where a benchmark's test
split is unlabelled we use the validation split, as lm-eval does. HellaSwag is
scored from the plain text file that llama.cpp's own helper script downloads;
the multiple-choice benchmarks are converted from their public releases
(\texttt{datasets} 5.0.0) into llama.cpp's binary multiple-choice format by a
small converter written against the engine's parser (\texttt{uint32} task
count, a table of absolute byte offsets, and per task the question string, the
answer strings, one \texttt{int32} label per answer and an empty \texttt{mc2}
block); a converted file is accepted only if the engine loads every task
without a read error. The converter (\texttt{converti\_multiple\_choice.py}, SHA-256
\texttt{16c903ffe7e6a6d493920853a2197874c51e88568345734cf71b8268771f529b}) and
the SHA-256 of every converted file are released with the supplementary
material.

\paragraph{PIQA is a declared subset.} The official PIQA validation split has
1,838 items. The Parquet mirror we used (\texttt{gimmaru/piqa}, the only
loader-free copy available to \texttt{datasets} 5.x) serves a 1,000-row
validation split; we verified after the runs, against the official
\texttt{dev.jsonl}, that all 1,000 rows and labels are present in the
official split with no duplicates, so the file is a proper subset of the
validation set (54\%), of unknown selection rule, and not a prefix. We report
PIQA as a 1,000-item subset, exclude it from every ``run whole'' count, and
note that it is a tie in both comparisons.

\paragraph{Prompt templates.} The template decides the number, so we state it
per benchmark, zero-shot throughout except TruthfulQA MC1 (lm-eval's MMLU default is 5-shot).
MMLU: ``\texttt{The following are multiple choice questions (with answers)
about \{subject\}.\textbackslash n\textbackslash n\{question\}\textbackslash nA.
\{a\}\textbackslash nB. \{b\}\textbackslash nC. \{c\}\textbackslash nD.
\{d\}\textbackslash nAnswer:}'', scored over the four answer strings
\texttt{A}, \texttt{B}, \texttt{C}, \texttt{D} (one token each, so per-token
normalisation is a no-op on MMLU). ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge and PIQA:
``\texttt{Question: \{question\}\textbackslash nAnswer:}'' over the option
texts. OpenBookQA: the bare question stem over the option texts. BoolQ:
``\texttt{\{passage\}\textbackslash nQuestion: \{question\}?\textbackslash
nAnswer:}'' over \texttt{no}/\texttt{yes}. An earlier BoolQ template without
the \texttt{Question:}/\texttt{Answer:} scaffold put the 397B ternary file
below chance (47.19\%); those runs are among the discarded logs. HellaSwag and
Winogrande use the engine's own scorers on the files its helper scripts
download. TruthfulQA MC1: lm-eval's fixed six-example QA preamble
(\texttt{tasks/truthfulqa/truthfulqa\_mc1.yaml}), copied verbatim and
prepended to every question, scored over the \texttt{mc1\_targets} option
strings (4 to 13 per question); it is the one corpus in this paper that is
not zero-shot, because it has no zero-shot form in the literature we could
point to instead.

\paragraph{Commands.} Every figure in this section comes from one of the five
invocations below, with \texttt{MODEL} the GGUF file and no other flag.
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
# HellaSwag: every task of the file (random order, seed 1, without replacement)
llama-perplexity -m MODEL -ngl 999 -c 4096 --hellaswag \
    --hellaswag-tasks 100000 -f hellaswag_val_full.txt
# Multiple choice: every task of the file, in order
llama-perplexity -m MODEL -ngl 999 -c 8192 -np 8 --multiple-choice \
    --multiple-choice-tasks 100000 -f FILE.bin
# TruthfulQA MC1: up to 13 options per question (six-shot preamble baked
# into FILE.bin); batch reduced to fit the 397B file's compute buffers
llama-perplexity -m MODEL -ngl 999 -c 3328 -np 13 -b 256 -ub 256 \
    --multiple-choice --multiple-choice-tasks 100000 -f FILE.bin
# Winogrande: all 1267 items
llama-perplexity -m MODEL -ngl 999 -c 4096 --winogrande \
    --winogrande-tasks 100000 -f winogrande-debiased-eval.csv
# Perplexity, as in the previous version
llama-perplexity -m MODEL -ngl 999 -c 4096 -b 2048 -f wiki.test.raw
\end{verbatim}}
Two flags are not details. llama.cpp's HellaSwag scorer always draws tasks at
random without replacement (fixed seed 1) until the requested count or the
file is exhausted; asking for at least as many tasks as the file holds
therefore scores every item, in random order. For queueing we ran HellaSwag
on 21 sequential pieces of the same file (500 tasks $=$ 3,000 lines each, the
last of 42 tasks, cut at task boundaries; \texttt{--hellaswag-tasks 500 -f
PIECE.txt}); each task is scored independently, so the per-item outcomes and
the corpus total are those of the single command above, and the piece files
and the cutting script are released with the supplementary material. The multiple-choice scorer
assigns one sequence per answer option, and ARC contains five-option items:
with the default \texttt{-np 4} the run stops at the first such item while
reporting a result, so \texttt{-np 8} is required, and \texttt{-c 8192} with
\texttt{-np 8} gives the same 1024 tokens per sequence as \texttt{-c 4096}
with \texttt{-np 4}. TruthfulQA MC1 has up to 13 options per question (mode
5, over the standard 817-item \texttt{multiple\_choice} split), so it was run
with \texttt{-np 13} and, to leave headroom for the 397B file's compute
buffers alongside its resident weights, \texttt{-c 3328} and \texttt{-b}/\texttt{-ub}
reduced to 256; the discarded logs include one attempt each at a wider batch
and at \texttt{-np 16}. A multiple-choice log is accepted only if its last scored
task number equals the task count in its header.

\paragraph{Metric.} From the engine's source: HellaSwag scores each ending by
its total log-probability divided by its token count and picks the maximum;
the multiple-choice scorer applies the same per-token-normalised
log-probability to each answer (for MMLU, whose answers are single letters,
this reduces to the raw log-probability); Winogrande scores the sentence
completed with either candidate. This is per-token normalisation, not
lm-eval's per-character \texttt{acc\_norm}, zero-shot with the templates
above --- except TruthfulQA MC1's fixed six-shot preamble, which has no
established zero-shot form --- rather than lm-eval's 5-shot MMLU, and the numbers are comparable
between the four files of this paper, not with external leaderboards.

\paragraph{Execution and aggregation.} HellaSwag, MMLU, BoolQ and ARC-Easy
were split into sequential chunks of the corpus (HellaSwag in 21 chunks of 500
tasks, the last of 42) and scored through a shared queue,
one worker per GPU; a failed job returns to the queue and every raw log is
kept, including the discarded ones (the raw logs of the cloud run, 38 per
file, are released with the supplementary material). A chunk counts only if
its log is complete.
The corpus figure is $\sum_c \mathrm{round}(p_c n_c) / \sum_c n_c$ over
chunks, with a Wilson 95\% interval on the corpus total; perplexity is the
engine's own estimate with its standard error. In the HellaSwag validation
file the ActivityNet items are tasks 1--3,243 and the WikiHow items tasks
3,244--10,042; the chunk boundary nearest to that split is at task 3,500, so
the two source halves we report are items 1--3,500 (all 3,243 ActivityNet
plus the first 257 WikiHow) and items 3,501--10,042 (WikiHow only). That
split is descriptive and post hoc, not a tested hypothesis.

\paragraph{Decision rule and multiplicity.} The four files were scored on
identical items in identical order (the multiple-choice scorer runs in file
order; the HellaSwag scorer's seed is fixed; Winogrande logs the choice per
item), and every raw log yields the per-item outcome (directly for
Winogrande, as the difference of consecutive cumulative-accuracy rows for the
others), so the design is paired and we test it as such: for every benchmark
we report the discordant counts, the exact two-sided McNemar $p$ and a paired
Wald 95\% interval on the difference, exactly as Table~\ref{tab:suite} does.
There are twenty primary comparisons (ten corpora $\times$ two pairs); a
Bonferroni correction over the ten corpora of one pair sets
$\alpha = 0.05/10 = 0.005$, and we call a benchmark \emph{separated} when its
paired $p$ is below that. We keep the previous version's rule --- two files
whose Wilson 95\% intervals do not overlap --- as a conservative descriptor
(it corresponds to roughly $z > 2.77$ on an unpaired test, comparable to the
corrected threshold), and no longer call an unseparated benchmark a ``tie'':
we say what the paired interval excludes.

benchmark harness's model registry, and that script sets

launcher sets rather than a logged fact. On that reading the two evaluations
measure the same bytes under two different second-plane
configurations, and neither is the tail-only configuration whose paired
confirmation Section~\ref{sec:depthrule} reports. The file-level results
stand as measurements of the file as served in each setting; the depth-rule
evidence stands on the engine-switch measurements of

contain is a whole-corpus evaluation of the tail-only configuration, or of the

tensors is \texttt{APEX-MTP-I-Quality} (22.09~GiB), from the same public
repository as the rival. Table~\ref{tab:bench35} therefore tests the ternary

was not run on these corpora. Tony's base was verified
from the GGUF metadata (architecture, expert count, the \texttt{nextn}
tensors), not from the file name, and its rival is the file of the same public
repository whose tensor set matches (with the MTP head; the closest file
without it, \texttt{APEX-I-Compact}, is 15.40~GiB and lacks a tensor, and was
not run). We do not
compare the 35B files with the 397B files: different sizes, different
questions.

do not say the ternary file is never worse, but say instead that no corpus

source shows the same sign on
both halves ($+3.17$ on items 1--3,500, $+1.88$ on the WikiHow-only remainder),

\subsection{A quantizer-level dissociation: reasoning survives RTN, not GPTQ}
\label{sec:dissociation}

The result we did not expect from this suite concerns the quantization
\emph{algorithm} rather than the file. Our forge has produced, at the same
bit-rate and the same two-plane layout, first-plane variants quantized by
plain optimal-scale RTN and by GPTQ against generic-text calibration
Hessians. On every proxy we had trusted so far, GPTQ wins: better
perplexity, and the no-think task rates of Table~\ref{tab:suite}. With
reasoning \emph{enabled}, the ranking inverts. The RTN build produces sane
chains of thought and passes all three hard verifiable probes (multi-step
percentage arithmetic; a classic kinship-counting trap; character-level
string reversal). The GPTQ builds, on the same probes, degenerate into
unterminated loops of the thinking-close token and never converge to an
answer --- a failure invisible to perplexity and to no-think task rate alike.

This dissociation is consistent with the emerging picture of
calibration-distribution mismatch in low-bit reasoning models. Quantization
is known to damage reasoning disproportionately at low bit-rates
\citep{liu2025quantreasoning}, with looping and non-termination documented as
characteristic failure modes of extreme low-bit inference
\citep{alimaskina2026lowbit}, and quantized reasoning models systematically
inflating their chain-of-thought length without gaining accuracy
\citep{lotfi2026overthink}. The remedy reported in that literature is
distributional: calibrating on reasoning-style data rather than generic text
recovers large fractions of the loss \citep{liu2025quantreasoning}, and
calibrating on the model's \emph{own} generated reasoning traces recovers up
to 25.5 points at 1.58 bits \citep{wang2026scaleq}; practitioner recipes for
1.58-bit reasoning models likewise survive only by protecting the components
that generic pipelines damage \citep{unsloth2025r1dynamic}. Our RTN/GPTQ pair
isolates the same variable within a single forge (the shipped file's first
plane is RTN on layers 0--43 and GPTQ on 44--59, Section~\ref{sec:rival}):
GPTQ minimises error
\emph{on the calibration distribution} and displaces it off-distribution ---
and long self-generated reasoning traces are precisely off-distribution with
respect to a generic text corpus, while RTN's error is
distribution-agnostic. We state this as the consistent explanation, not a
demonstrated mechanism; the discriminating experiment (re-forging the GPTQ
planes against self-generated reasoning traces, ScaleQ-style) was not
performed for this version, and its outcome would be falsifiable in the same
way as Section~\ref{sec:refutations}'s
hypotheses. Until it is done, the shipped file runs with reasoning disabled in
production, and Table~\ref{tab:suite} is a no-think table by construction.

\section{The routing-margin distribution}
\label{sec:margin}

\paragraph{Definition.} For a token routed to $k$ of $E$ experts \citep{switch}, the margin is the gap
between the $k$-th and $(k+1)$-th router score --- the distance to the nearest
routing flip. ReMoE \citep{zhu2026remoe} defines it in probability space as the premise of a
stability lemma; \citet{gu2026routeflip} defines it in logit space and collapses it
to a single AUC. Neither reports its distribution; \citet{chen2026certainhead} reports
a token-averaged mean over depth, which to our knowledge is the only measured
summary of it in the literature. We measure the distribution on the 397B's
captured router probabilities (16{,}640 tokens $\times$ 60 layers, the same capture
that feeds the forge's Hessians).

\paragraph{The distribution.} The median margin is $10.1\times$ narrower than the uniform
share $1/E$; the 1st percentile is $806\times$ narrower; 2.95\% of tokens sit within
one bf16 ULP of the 8th/9th boundary --- for these, the routing decision is
below the numerical noise floor of the arithmetic that computes it. The
distribution is right-skewed, mean/median 1.76: the mean curve published in
prior work sits 76\% above the typical token, which is precisely the
regime where a mean reassures and a median warns.

\paragraph{What follows and what does not.} The qualitative half of this measurement
was asserted, on synthetic weights, by the author of sglang PR \#35916
\citep{sglang35916} --- batch-invariant routing motivated by adjacent scores within one ULP on this
very topology --- who lacked hardware for the real model; this section
supplies the measurement. What does \emph{not} follow is the tempting link to
Section~\ref{sec:refutations}'s damage profile: route-flip counts anti-correlate with damage
(the all-layers configuration flips $5\times$ more routes than the worst single
layer at $3.5\times$ less damage), and the one published flip classifier sits at
chance (AUC 0.490). Fragile margins are a fact of the router; they are not,
by count alone, the mechanism of the correction paradox.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Extreme low-bit post-training quantization.} The methods that
define the state of the art below two bits on dense models --- additive
codebook quantization (AQLM, \citealp{egiazarian2024aqlm}), lattice codebooks
with incoherence processing (QuIP\#, \citealp{tseng2024quipsharp}), trellis
coding (QTIP, \citealp{tseng2024qtip}), and the fine-tuning framework that
improves on all of them (PV-Tuning, \citealp{malinovskii2024pvtuning}) ---
reach lower error per bit than the ternary primitive we use, and the rotation
line (QuaRot, \citealp{ashkboos2024quarot}; SpinQuant,
\citealp{liu2025spinquant}) removes outliers before quantizing at all. None of
them is measured here, and the omission is a scope statement rather than a
claim: none has a ternary MoE kernel in the engine that serves our files, and
the rival rule of Section~\ref{sec:rival} restricts the comparison to public
quantizations of the same checkpoint runnable by the same engine. Our
positioning is that of a deployment format, not of a rate-distortion optimum;
what transfers from this paper to those methods is the depth question, not the
primitive.

\paragraph{Ternary post-training quantization.} The TQ1\_0 packing we build on
was contributed to llama.cpp for TriLM- and BitNet-style ternary models
\citep{compilade2024tq1}. PTQTP \citep{xiao2025ptqtp} introduces
the dual trit-plane decomposition we build on, applied uniformly, with no
per-layer analysis. Tied Trit-Planes \citep{grella2026tiedtritplanes} is the closest work:
two ternary planes on a disk-streamed MoE, same primitive, same deployment
constraint --- and it reports, without explanation, the same proxy/fidelity
dissociation we measure (its higher-reconstruction-error variant scores
\emph{better} on MMLU). Its ladder is over tensor classes and, by its own
statement, ``localizes sensitivity to the final cumulative bundle, not to a
single component''; per-layer localisation is precisely what we add. BitNet
\citep{ma2024bitnet} trains ternary from scratch and keeps embeddings
high-precision; our setting is post-hoc, on weights never meant for ternary.

\paragraph{Native ternary models.} Ternary Bonsai 2 27B
\citep{prismml2026bonsai2} is a dense 27B hybrid-attention model released in
ternary form --- weights in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ in a fixed blockwise Hadamard basis
with FP16 group scales, 1.76 bits/weight as shipped --- that reports 98.2\% of
its full-precision base's thinking-mode average (reasoning effort
\texttt{xhigh}) over a 20-benchmark generative suite. It is
a reference point for what ternary weights can retain when the ternary
representation is produced by the model's own vendor with a dedicated
pipeline, its own rotated basis and its own kernels; it is not a comparison
target for this paper. Our files are post-hoc conversions of published MoE
checkpoints by a third party, our evaluation is log-likelihood scoring of the
served file, and our question is \emph{where} a correction may land rather
than what a ternary model can reach. The two settings share the ternary
alphabet but not the format --- Bonsai's group-wise packing with FP16 scales
every 128 weights in a blockwise Hadamard basis against our unrotated
256-weight TQ1\_0 block --- and bracket it from opposite ends. Its retention
figure is a reference point for what the ternary alphabet can hold when the
representation is chosen at training time; it is not a ceiling commensurable
with our tables, which report log-likelihood scoring in no-think mode with no
full-precision reference for the largest model.

\paragraph{Non-additivity of compression error.} EvoPress \citep{sieberling2025evopress} opens on the observation that per-layer independence fails and
that removing \emph{more} blocks can help; its search machinery (fitness = whole-model
KL) subsumes our selection procedure. What it explicitly does not do is
isolate single catastrophic layers or offer a mechanism. QDrop \citep{wei2022qdrop}
and Quant-Noise \citep{fan2021quantnoise} establish the non-monotone shape at calibration
time --- full quantization-aware noise \emph{worse} than none, partial better than
both --- which is the same shape as our cancellation, in a different regime.

\paragraph{Mixed precision within MoE.} The expert-wise bit-allocation literature
(MoPEQ \citep{chittyvenkata2025mopeq}, AlphaQ \citep{alphaq}, MC-MoE \citep{mcmoe}, GEMQ \citep{gemq}, BitsMoE \citep{bitsmoe}) is uniformly monotone in its
assumptions: the only failure mode contemplated is mis-estimating importance.
The phenomenon we isolate is visible, unremarked, in Table~1 of GEMQ \citep{gemq}
(expert-level mixed precision losing to uniform at matched bits on
Qwen3-30B-A3B, $-9.89$ acc at 2.0 bpe) and named in HiFloat4 \citep{mak2026hifloat4}:
restoring one component to higher precision scores \emph{below} uniform FP4.
Table~1 of AWQ \citep{lin2024awq} shows the selection-criterion analogue: protecting the wrong 1\%
is worse than protecting nothing. Our framing --- heterogeneous fidelity, not
insufficient fidelity --- is the synthesis these scattered rows point to.

\paragraph{Routing fragility.} The margin at the top-$k$ boundary is defined twice and,
to our knowledge, never reported as a distribution: ReMoE
\citep[App.~C.2]{zhu2026remoe} as the unmeasured premise
of a stability lemma, in probability space; \citet{gu2026routeflip} in logit space,
collapsed to one AUC (and its margin$\to$harmful classifier sits at chance,
0.490 --- consistent with our flip-count anti-correlation). A token-averaged
mean curve exists \citep{chen2026certainhead}; percentiles do not, and the
mean-vs-median gap ($1.76\times$) is exactly why it matters. sglang PR \#35916
\citep{sglang35916} asserts the qualitative half --- adjacent scores within one bf16 ULP on this
very topology --- on synthetic weights, its author lacking hardware for the
real measurement; we supply it.

\paragraph{Depth structure.} The representational literature locates a shared,
language- and modality-independent semantic space in the \emph{intermediate}
layers \citep{wu2025semantichub}; our worst layer sits at 0.488 of depth, in
that region. We state only what we can cite: we are aware of no quantitative
prediction, in that literature, of a sign flip in corruption tolerance, and we
do not claim one. The
intervention literature, by contrast, is dominated by edge-sensitivity: GLU
activation spikes at early \emph{and late} layers \citep{yang2024gluspikes},
llama.cpp's own extra-bit heuristic on the first and last eighths, and layer-pruning work
finding deep layers most redundant \citep{shortgpt,gromov2024unreasonable}. The tension is real and stated: those
works measure activation magnitude or deletion tolerance; we measure
\emph{corruption} tolerance, and deletion-vs-corruption is exactly the distinction
the reconciliation requires (a deleted layer passes the residual through; a
corrupted one writes an actively wrong signal where the representation is
being formed). One prior intervention study reports a mid-depth sensitivity
peak \citep[activation injection, mean peak 54.9\%]{xu2025steering}.

\paragraph{Down-projection difficulty.} Known and named: D$^2$Quant \citep{yan2026d2quant}
calls it ``a well-known quantization bottleneck'' and engineers a dedicated
dual-scale quantizer. Community evidence on our very model family: unsloth's
v3 recipe placing 20 large FFN tensors at 2 bits measurably degraded coding.
What is not in the literature is the sign flip --- \texttt{down} catastrophic on the
mountain, \emph{helpful} in the tail --- nor any derivation from gated-activation
statistics (our attempt at one is refuted by measurement: kurtosis, effective
rank and delivered error all fail to track the damage).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Two models, one family.} Both are Qwen-lineage MoEs (35B and 397B) with
  shared tokenizer and expert topology conventions. The depth bracket is
  measured twice, not derived; a third family is the obvious next
  falsification. A dense-model forge exists as an artefact and was run on one
  target; we do not report a result from it in this version, because the runs
  we have do not reach a depth-resolved measurement --- at this bit-rate the
  dense FFNs fail globally first --- and we prefer to state that than to
  quote a number from an incomplete experiment. If it holds, it would make the
  depth rule a property of \emph{corrections to survivable quantizations}
  rather than of quantization per se, consistent with the MoE-robustness
  literature \citep{kim2023moqe}.
\item \textbf{Log-likelihood benchmarks only; generative benchmarks
  deferred.} The depth-rule confirmations are paired logit-level comparisons
  plus verifiable-answer functional probes, and the file-level evaluation of
  Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} scores log-likelihoods of given choices with
  llama.cpp's own scorers, on complete corpora. Benchmarks that require
  generation (GSM8K, HumanEval, MMLU-Pro, GPQA, generative TruthfulQA) need a
  harness driving a server rather than this protocol; they appear in no table
  of this paper and are deferred to a future version. TruthfulQA's other
  format, MC1, has no generation step --- it scores log-likelihoods of given
  choices exactly like the other corpora --- so it is not deferred and
  appears in Tables~\ref{tab:bench397} and~\ref{tab:bench35} instead. The metric is
  per-token-normalised (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}), so the figures compare
  the four files of this paper with each other and not with external
  leaderboards.
\item \textbf{The engine is archived, not versioned.} The TQ1\_0 Vulkan
  kernels are upstream and the forge scripts are public, but the two-plane
  \emph{loader} is not in any public repository, and the fork's git history
  was not kept: the working copies were made without it and the binary
  reports no hash. The base commit was recovered afterwards by file matching
  (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}), not from a record, and we state it as such.
  Without the loader no number in Table~\ref{tab:bench397} can be
  regenerated, so the engine used for every run is released with the
  supplementary material as a diff against that base commit (19 files) and as
  a diff against the upstream merge commit of the TQ1\_0 Vulkan path, each
  with its SHA-256; one project-name string in comments and in three
  debugging environment-variable names was replaced for anonymity, and the
  replacement is declared there. The
  kernels were written for AMD RDNA; on the NVIDIA GPUs of
  Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} they run correctly
  (Table~\ref{tab:crosscheck}) --- with one declared paired perplexity offset
  of $0.96\%$ on the two-plane file (Section~\ref{sec:crosscheck}) ---
  but are not optimised (about 0.6~s per HellaSwag task on the 397B), and there
  is no CUDA path for TQ1\_0.
\item \textbf{What the benchmarked files are, and what was not run.} The
  397B file carries second-plane tensors on all 60 layers, so
  Table~\ref{tab:bench397} measures it with the correction applied everywhere,
  while the task suite of Section~\ref{sec:tasks} was served with the second
  plane disabled (Section~\ref{sec:rival}); we found this while preparing this
  revision and state it rather than re-run either evaluation. No whole-corpus
  evaluation of the tail-only configuration of Section~\ref{sec:depthrule}
  exists in this paper: the depth rule rests on the per-layer engine-switch
  measurements and the paired logit-level confirmation, not on
  Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks}. The 35B file of Table~\ref{tab:bench35}
  carries a single plane and no correction, and the two-plane 35B file of
  Sections~\ref{sec:paradox}--\ref{sec:depthrule} was not run on these corpora.
\item \textbf{The 35B file is not published.} Tony-v4-B (15.47~GiB) is not yet
  in a public repository, so Table~\ref{tab:bench35} and the 35B cross-check
  row cannot be regenerated by a reader; the regenerability claim of
  Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} covers the 397B pair, whose two files are both
  public. We state this rather than weaken the table.
\item \textbf{Cross-vendor coverage is partial.} The two GPU vendors were
  compared on perplexity and Winogrande for the 397B pair and on HellaSwag for
  the 35B file. The three benchmarks that separate the 397B pair (BoolQ,
  HellaSwag, MMLU) were measured on NVIDIA only, and the item-level
  disagreement between backends on the shared items is large even where the
  totals agree (Table~\ref{tab:crosscheck}).
\item \textbf{Context and scope of the benchmark runs.} Context is 4096 tokens
  (8192 over eight sequences for multiple choice, i.e.\ 1024 tokens per
  sequence). \texttt{llama-perplexity} aborts on any task whose tokens exceed
  the per-sequence budget, and each of the two BoolQ pieces reached its header
  task count (2,000 and 1,270; 3,270 in total) with no ``does not fit'' line,
  so no passage was truncated or skipped; the longest BoolQ passage in the
  converted file is 823 words. Reasoning is off in every measurement. The
  smaller MTP-less sibling of the 35B rival (15.40~GiB, one tensor fewer) was
  not run, so the rival rule was satisfied on tensor set rather than on size
  alone. The internal
  task suite remains private, as in the previous version.
\item \textbf{The selection confound is quantified, not closed.} Frequency-selection
  wastes 42.6\% of plane-2 impact in the head and its correlational profile
  tracks damage at $+0.788$; the causal test refutes it as \emph{the} mechanism,
  but a Hessian-impact-selected reforge (the artefact exists; the run was not
  performed for this version) is required before selection can be fully
  dismissed as a contributor to the magnitude, as opposed to the sign.
\item \textbf{No mechanism.} We offer a rule and eight dead hypotheses. The
  representational literature \citep{wu2025semantichub} is consistent with the
  region in which we measure the worst layer and predicts none of its
  consequences quantitatively. We consider
  naming this openly a feature of the paper, not a gap in it.
\item \textbf{Planning is the weakest family, and reasoning is off.} The
  planning family of the task suite is the lowest-scoring for \emph{both}
  397B files with reasoning disabled (6/10 against 2/10, $n = 10$) --- the
  suite comparison says little about which file plans better, only that
  neither does it well at this bit-rate without chain-of-thought. And
  reasoning is disabled in the shipped configuration
  precisely because of the GPTQ dissociation of
  Section~\ref{sec:dissociation}: until the reasoning-trace recalibration
  closes that gap, the task-level claims of Table~\ref{tab:suite} are
  no-think claims and must not be read as more.
\item \textbf{One quantization primitive.} Everything is TQ1\_0-shaped ternary with
  two-level scale search and RTN or GPTQ; whether the depth rule survives a change
  of primitive (say, trellis-coded \citep{tseng2024qtip} or lattice
  \citep{tseng2024quipsharp} quantization at matched bpw)
  is untested. A preliminary result on real weights says the change of
  primitive is worth forging: an exact tail-biting Viterbi encoder over
  QTIP's 4096-state bitshift trellis \citep{tseng2024qtip} (56~bytes per
  256-weight block $=$ 1.75~bpw
  exact), run on \texttt{blk.29.attn\_gate} of the 397B (dequantized from
  the Q8\_0 reference; 4096 blocks sampled uniformly at seed~0 --- a
  declared subset on which the RTN baseline matches its full-tensor value
  to four decimals), reaches relative error $0.3473$ at 1.75~bpw against
  $0.4420$ for optimal-scale ternary RTN at 1.69~bpw: $-38.3\%$ MSE at
  near-matched rate. The measurement behaves like its theory: the gaussian
  simulation ($0.3329$) transfers to the real tensor with the same
  ${\sim}1\%$ excess that RTN shows over its Lloyd bound
  ($0.4362 \to 0.4420$); tail-biting converges on 4093/4096 blocks in at
  most three extra passes; the packed 56-byte payload decodes bit-exactly
  to the same error; and QTIP's compute-based (1MAD) codebook
  \citep{tseng2024qtip} costs
  ${\sim}3\%$ MSE that an 8~KiB lookup table would recover. This is a
  pre-test on one tensor, not a forged model: whether the tensor-level
  $-38\%$ survives GPTQ, a full forge, and --- the question this paper
  makes precise --- the depth rule, is exactly what remains untested.
\end{itemize}

\section{Changes from the previous version}
\label{sec:changes}


This is a revision of a manuscript previously submitted to TMLR. The previous
version reported a complete standardised evaluation as a debt and, in its
benchmark ladder, described its HellaSwag figures as measured on the same
2,108-item \emph{prefix} of the validation set. That description was wrong.
llama.cpp's HellaSwag scorer always draws tasks at random without replacement
from the whole file (a fixed-seed generator, seed 1), so the 2,108 items were a
random subset in random order --- not a prefix --- and one of the two runs had
been launched for 4,000 tasks and stopped at 2,108. Because the draw sequence
does not depend on how many tasks are requested, both files were scored on the
same 2,108 items and the comparison was paired, as claimed; but the sample was
neither the one described nor one a reader could regenerate without the seed,
the engine version and the interruption point. Winogrande (all 1,267 items) and
perplexity (all 72 chunks) were complete in the previous version and are
unchanged.

This version replaces the partial figure with complete runs of nine public
benchmark corpora, and a declared subset of a tenth, on four files, with sources, splits, item counts, exact
commands and hardware (Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks}). Three further corrections
follow from the new data. First, the previous version stated that perplexity
ranks the two 397B files \emph{backwards} (7.5903 against 7.5438 on the AMD
machine); on the NVIDIA runs the two files are indistinguishable (7.5180
against 7.5173), and the earlier gap was inside its own error bars, so the
claim is now the weaker and correct one --- perplexity does not separate two
files that eight points on BoolQ and twenty-three on the task suite do
separate. Second, the context length of the 72-chunk perplexity runs is 4096,
not the 2048 of the paired 30-chunk measurements; Section~\ref{sec:setup} now
states it per table. Third, the prose accompanying the task-suite table had
drifted from the table (it described an aggregate gap of 19.2 points, a
long-context family the baseline won, and a planning family tied at 20\%); the
table is the frozen record and the prose now matches it (72 fixtures, 144
scheduled pairs, 143 valid). We also declare limitations the previous version
did not state: the git history of our llama.cpp fork was not preserved (its
base commit has been recovered by file matching and the engine is released as
a diff, Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) and the two-plane loader is not yet in a
public repository;
generative benchmarks are deferred to a future version; the 35B file of
Section~\ref{sec:bench35} is not yet published; and --- found while preparing
this revision by reading the shipped file's tensor table against the engine's
default --- the 397B file carries second-plane tensors on all 60 layers, so
the public-benchmark runs (no environment variable set) applied the second
plane everywhere while the task-suite runs were served with it disabled
(Sections~\ref{sec:rival} and~\ref{sec:limitations}). One cross-check of the
previous version was misattributed: the 35B HellaSwag home run was made on the
AMD desktop APU, not on a laptop RTX~4060 (Section~\ref{sec:crosscheck}).
